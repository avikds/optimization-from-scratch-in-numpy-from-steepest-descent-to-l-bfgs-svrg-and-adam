Optimization from Scratch in NumPy: From Steepest Descent to L-BFGS, SVRG and Adam

How it was built

Part 1: The Test Bed

Three objectives every solver in the project will face: quadratics whose condition number you choose, the curved valley of eq. 8.19, and L2-regularized logistic regression on breast-cancer data, wrapped in a ledger that charges each solver for every pass over the data.

Part 2: First-Order Methods

Section 8.2: fixed steps and the limit eta < 2/lambda_max, exact line search and the kappa-dependent rate of eq. 8.29, Armijo backtracking, and heavy-ball and Nesterov momentum, with iteration counts that grow as kappa and as its square root.

Part 3: Second-Order Methods

Section 8.3: Newton's method and its failure on non-convex functions, trust regions with Tikhonov damping, the BFGS updates, a Wolfe line search, and L-BFGS through the two-loop recursion.

Part 4: Stochastic Gradients and Variance Reduction

Sections 8.4.1-8.4.5: the LMS algorithm of Figure 8.16, learning-rate schedules and the Robbins-Monro conditions, minibatch SGD with Polyak-Ruppert averaging, and then SVRG and SAGA, which give finite-sum problems a linear rate.

Part 5: Adaptive Learning Rates

Section 8.4.6: diagonal preconditioners from AdaGrad to Adam with bias correction, and then the failure the book warns about, reproduced on the Reddi counterexample, with AMSGrad, Yogi and ADOPT as the proposed repairs.

Part 6: Constraints and Proximal Methods

Sections 8.5 and 8.6: projected gradient on a box, an active-set QP with KKT certificates on the book's diamond, a simplex solver that meets its exponential worst case, ISTA for the lasso, and BinaryConnect against ProxQuant on a problem small enough to brute-force.

Part 7: Bound Optimization and the Scoreboard

Section 8.7: EM for a Gaussian mixture on Old Faithful, the collapsing variance, MAP-EM and Figure 8.26(b), and then every solver in the project raced on one ledger.
